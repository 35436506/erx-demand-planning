Choosing models by product group

ABC-XYZ, intermittent items, out-of-sample best fit — policies for the whole portfolio

Stage 2 · Core techniquesClass date: ILO5

By the end of this session you will be able to

  3. Use Croston/SBA/TSB for intermittent items and understand what that forecast means

  5. Compare top-down and bottom-up; ask the right questions when software reports 'best fit'

Company corner

When the software says 'best fit' — The right questions for the software vendor — and for yourself.

Case · MTS or MTO for OEM seasoning powder (H03)?
  • H03 is sold in BATCHES of 500 kg to 2 OEM customers. Over 36 months: 75% of months have no order; when there is one, it is 4–12 batches.
  • Production lead time 6 weeks; imported materials 8 weeks; shelf life 360 days.
  • Sales wants '10 batches always in stock' so customers never wait. Finance complains H03 inventory ties up 400 million.
  • Croston/SBA forecasts ~2–3 batches/month.
Discussion questions
  1. Does a forecast of 2–3 batches/month help decide how much to hold?
  2. Compare: MTS (hold stock), MTO (make to order, 6-week lead time), hybrid (hold materials, not finished goods)
  3. Which information from the OEM customers helps more than any model?
  4. Propose a policy for H03 in 3 sentences.

Exit ticket · self-check

Answer in the last 2–3 minutes of the session. The instructor goes through the answers in class — record how many you got right.

  1. Why is K02 classed Z by raw CV but Y after deseasonalising?
  2. What does ADI = 3 mean?
  3. Croston forecasts 15 cans/month for H02. Does that mean holding 15 cans is enough?
  4. When is top-down better than bottom-up?
  5. One question you would ask when the software reports a 'best fit' model.
