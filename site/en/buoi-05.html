Seasonality & the Lunar New Year (Tết) effect

Seasonal indices, Holt-Winters and forecasting when Tết moves month

Stage 2 · Core techniquesClass date: ILO4

By the end of this session you will be able to

  1. Compute normalised seasonal indices and read the seasonal amplitude
  2. Distinguish additive and multiplicative seasonality; deseasonalise for a fair comparison
  3. Forecast 12 months with trend × seasonal index and with Holt-Winters
  4. Handle Tết falling in January or February using shopping-window shares
  5. Build the company event calendar and decide on pre-building when the peak exceeds capacity
  6. Challenge 'Tết is early, so January will surely double' with numbers

Learning outcomes: ILO4 (modelling seasonality and calendar effects) · ILO7 (pre-build, capacity)

Company corner

Tết is early, Sales says January will double — Telling facts, opinions and assumptions apart as the peak season approaches.

Case · 'Tết is 3 weeks early, January must double!'
  • October 2026 meeting. The Sales Director: 'Tết 2027 is almost 2 weeks earlier than this year, distributors will load up in January — fish sauce in January must be double a normal month!'
  • Your numbers: the 2027 normal level ≈ 9,887 cases/month; the average Tết portion ≈ 0.50 of a normal month.
  • 2025 (Tết on 29 Jan, similar to 2027): January was about 50–60% above the normal level.
  • The bottling line can do at most ~40,300 cases/month for the whole sauce group.
Discussion questions
  1. Is 'January will double' a fact, an opinion or an assumption? Which year in the history would test it?
  2. Based on the data, what range is reasonable for January 2027? For February?
  3. If we accept 'double' and it is wrong, what happens to February–March inventory?
  4. What 'insurance' would you propose without fully believing the doubled number?

Key points to remember
  • Seasonality is information: compute indices on the series with events removed
  • Choosing the right form (additive/multiplicative) matters more than fine-tuning parameters
  • The Lunar New Year must be modelled separately — pool Dec–Feb, then split by the Tết calendar
