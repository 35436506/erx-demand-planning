Exponential Smoothing & rolling forecasts

SES, Holt, Solver — and why KPIs must be measured at the right lag

Stage 2 · Core techniquesClass date: ILO3

By the end of this session you will be able to

  1. Explain SES in one sentence: new forecast = old forecast + α × error
  2. Build SES and Holt with recursive formulas in Excel; understand the damped trend
  3. Choose parameters with Solver on TRAIN, evaluate on TEST and compare with benchmarks
  4. Run a monthly rolling forecast and measure error by lag
  5. Tie KPIs to the planning time fences
  6. Handle requests to change numbers inside the frozen zone

Company corner

When Sales wants to change the number inside the frozen zone — Plan change rules — who may change, by how much, who pays the cost.

Case · 'There's a big order next week, change November's number!'
  • 20 October 2026. November's schedule for the packet-noodle line is frozen and the packaging has arrived.
  • GT Sales calls: 'A Mekong Delta distributor has just said they'll take an extra 6,000 cases of N01 for their own programme — raise November's forecast.'
  • Factory: November already includes 6 days of maintenance and runs at full capacity; another 6,000 cases = overtime + rescheduling N02.
  • History: last year this distributor announced a 'big order' 3 times; only 1 became a real order.
Discussion questions
  1. What kind of information is this: fact, opinion or assumption? What evidence would upgrade it?
  2. Three options and their trade-offs (service, cost, 180-day noodle inventory)
  3. Who has the authority to approve changes inside the frozen zone? What should the process be?
  4. What do you record in the assumption log?

Groups of 3. Finish with: one decision + conditions + an owner.

RoleObjectiveOpening line
Sales Manager GTHave stock for the big distributor, don't lose the order to a competitor“The competitor is offering a better price — if we don't have stock we lose the customer for good.”
Production shift leaderKeep the schedule stable, avoid costly overtime and changeovers“Changing the schedule the week before the run costs half a shift in changeover — who pays for that?”

Key points to remember
  • Exponential smoothing: correct the forecast by a share of the error — α is chosen from the data
  • Holt for trends; damping is usually safer — but use it only when it beats the benchmark
  • Measure KPIs at the decision's lag; keep every forecast version
