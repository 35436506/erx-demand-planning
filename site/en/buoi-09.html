Session 9 / 10

From forecast to inventory and S&OP

Safety stock, early warning, shelf life, capacity and 5 KPIs

Stage 3 · Process & decisionsClass date: ILO7

By the end of this session you will be able to

  1. Compute safety stock and the reorder point (ROP) from out-of-sample forecast error
  2. Explain the trade-off between service level and inventory capital
  3. Build an in-month early warning from the sales rate of the first 14 days
  4. Spot lots at shelf-life risk under the FEFO rule
  5. Compare unconstrained demand with capacity and propose options
  6. Convert volume into value and present 5 KPIs for the S&OP meeting

Learning outcomes: ILO7 (inventory, S&OP, decisions with trade-offs)

Exit ticket · self-check

Answer in the last 2–3 minutes of the session. The instructor goes through the answers in class — record how many you got right.

  1. Why should σ in the Safety Stock formula be the forecast error, not the standard deviation of demand?
  2. If the forecast improves by 20%, how does Safety Stock change?
  3. Going from 95% to 99% service level: why does capital rise faster?
  4. The run-rate ratio on day 14 = 0.67. What do you do this week?
  5. Capacity is short: does the Demand Planner change the demand number, or do something else?

Key points to remember
  • Safety stock pays for forecast error — a better forecast means capital released
  • Early warning and shelf life turn the forecast into action within the month
  • The capacity gap goes up to S&OP with options — don't change the demand number
