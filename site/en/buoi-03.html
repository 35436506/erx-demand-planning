Your first forecast and measuring error

Three benchmark methods, six metrics and the golden train/test rule

Stage 1 · FoundationsClass date: ILO3

By the end of this session you will be able to

  1. Build 3 benchmark forecasts: Naive, Seasonal Naive, Moving Average
  2. Split the data into train/test by time and evaluate on test only
  3. Compute ME (Bias), MAE, RMSE, MAPE, WMAPE, MASE — and say which question each metric answers
  4. Explain why MAPE 'explodes' on slow sellers and why there is no 'standard MAPE' for every SKU
  5. Distinguish accuracy from the cost of error
  6. Write a 5-line accuracy report for your manager

Learning outcomes: ILO3 (baseline forecasts and out-of-sample evaluation with suitable metrics)

Company corner

Accuracy in the meeting room — Who is judged on which KPI — and what behaviour those KPIs drive.

Case · 'MAPE below 10% for every SKU'
  • MBF's new Supply Chain Director sets a KPI for the Planning team: 'MAPE < 10% for every SKU, with a quarterly bonus'.
  • After 2 months you notice: colleagues start 'smoothing' the data too much, HoReCa items are merged into other codes to make the report look good, and everyone avoids forecasting new products.
  • Monthly report: 18/44 series pass; H01–H03 and K04 are 'red' every single month.
Discussion questions
  1. What behaviour does this KPI encourage? Which behaviour is harmful?
  2. Propose an alternative KPI set (at most 3 metrics), with measurement level, lag and benchmark
  3. How should intermittent items be evaluated?
  4. How would you present your proposal to the director in 60 seconds?

Key points to remember
  • Always have a benchmark: Naive for stable series, Seasonal Naive for seasonal series
  • Split train/test first; evaluate out of sample; report WMAPE + Bias
  • An honest forecast — a safety buffer is an inventory decision, not the forecast's job
