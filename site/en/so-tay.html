A 10-session programme balancing practice and academic rigour for Demand Planners.

⤓ PDF⤓ Word

1. Course overview

Demand Planning & Forecasting in Practice is a 10-session × 3-hour course for working professionals, balancing the technical foundations of forecasting (to international academic standards) with the day-to-day practice of a Demand Planner in a Vietnamese FMCG/F&B company. The whole course revolves around one fictional company — Mekong Bloom Foods (MBF) — with 36 months of data, built-in data 'ailments' and simulated meetings, so that every technique is tied to a decision.

1.1 Design philosophy

  • Decision first, model second. Each session opens with a real decision (how much to produce, is the promotion worth it, how much safety stock) before the technique.
  • By hand → interactive tool → formula. Learners work 2–3 rows by hand, drag the sliders in the Lab to see the behaviour, and only then generalise with formulas and Excel.
  • Out-of-sample evidence. Every model choice is evaluated on data not used to build the model and compared with the Naive benchmark — a rule that runs through the whole course.
  • One case, one flow. Homework HW1–HW9 are the milestones of the capstone project: by Session 10, learners already have a complete forecasting cycle.
  • Distinguish demand, forecast, plan and target. Demand Planning owns unconstrained demand; capacity and trade-offs belong to Supply Review and S&OP.

3.2 Detailed objectives for each session

Session 1 · Why forecast demand & forecasting hierarchy — From a forecast number to each department's decisions

  • Explain which decisions forecasting serves at each horizon (week, month, year)
  • Clearly distinguish demand, forecast, plan and target, and why mixing them up causes harm
  • Place forecasting in the planning chain: Forecast → S&OP → MPS → MRP, and the planning time fences
  • Compute the coefficient of variation CV at 4 levels and choose the right forecasting level for each decision
  • Recognise the bullwhip effect when each department forecasts on its own
  • Map departments – decisions – horizons for your own company

Learning outcomes: ILO1 (role of forecasting in the planning system) · foundation for ILO7 (link to S&OP and Production Planning) · Excel: B01_PhanCap_CV · Reading: LN Ch.1 (1.1–1.6); Case 1; FPP3 Ch.1

Session 2 · Understanding and cleaning demand data — From sales data to demand data you can trust

  • Distinguish orders – shipments – invoices – sell-out – demand and choose the right source
  • Read a time series through four components: level, trend, seasonality, noise
  • Use the mean, median, standard deviation, CV and IQR to detect anomalies
  • Classify anomalies: data error or real event — and treat each correctly
  • Restore demand during stock-outs, fix unit errors, join old–new codes, interpolate gaps
  • Keep an adjustment log: never delete the raw numbers; every change has a reason and evidence

Learning outcomes: ILO2 (prepare demand data, restore unconstrained demand, with an audit log) · Excel: B02_LamSach_DuLieu · Reading: LN Ch.8; LN 1.2; Case 8

Session 3 · Your first forecast and measuring error — Three benchmark methods, six metrics and the golden train/test rule

  • Build 3 benchmark forecasts: Naive, Seasonal Naive, Moving Average
  • Split the data into train/test by time and evaluate on test only
  • Compute ME (Bias), MAE, RMSE, MAPE, WMAPE, MASE — and say which question each metric answers
  • Explain why MAPE 'explodes' on slow sellers and why there is no 'standard MAPE' for every SKU
  • Distinguish accuracy from the cost of error
  • Write a 5-line accuracy report for your manager

Learning outcomes: ILO3 (baseline forecasts and out-of-sample evaluation with suitable metrics) · Excel: B03_DuBao_SaiSo · Reading: LN Ch.2, 7.4; Case 2, Case 7

Session 4 · Exponential Smoothing & rolling forecasts — SES, Holt, Solver — and why KPIs must be measured at the right lag

  • Explain SES in one sentence: new forecast = old forecast + α × error
  • Build SES and Holt with recursive formulas in Excel; understand the damped trend
  • Choose parameters with Solver on TRAIN, evaluate on TEST and compare with benchmarks
  • Run a monthly rolling forecast and measure error by lag
  • Tie KPIs to the planning time fences
  • Handle requests to change numbers inside the frozen zone

Learning outcomes: ILO3 (baseline forecasts, out-of-sample evaluation) · ILO6 (adjustment rules in the process) · Excel: B04_SES_Holt · Reading: LN Ch.3 (3.1–3.6); Case 3; FPP3 Ch.8

Session 5 · Seasonality & the Lunar New Year (Tết) effect — Seasonal indices, Holt-Winters and forecasting when Tết moves month

  • Compute normalised seasonal indices and read the seasonal amplitude
  • Distinguish additive and multiplicative seasonality; deseasonalise for a fair comparison
  • Forecast 12 months with trend × seasonal index and with Holt-Winters
  • Handle Tết falling in January or February using shopping-window shares
  • Build the company event calendar and decide on pre-building when the peak exceeds capacity
  • Challenge 'Tết is early, so January will surely double' with numbers

Learning outcomes: ILO4 (modelling seasonality and calendar effects) · ILO7 (pre-build, capacity) · Excel: B05_MuaVu_Tet · Reading: LN 1.2, 3.7; Case 3 (part C); FPP3 Ch.3, 8.3

Session 6 · Regression, price and promotions — Putting promotions into the forecast and challenging Marketing with numbers

  • Run multiple regression with LINEST/Data Analysis and read the coefficients, R², p-values
  • Choose explanatory variables that are KNOWN IN ADVANCE at the time of forecasting
  • Split the forecast into baseline + uplift; estimate the post-promotion dip
  • Test Marketing's proposal with the model and with gross margin
  • Understand cannibalisation between SKUs and the limits of monthly data
  • Forecast new products: channel fill + repeat sales

Learning outcomes: ILO4 (price, promotions, cannibalisation, NPD) · ILO6 (challenging Marketing's information) · Excel: B06_HoiQuy_KhuyenMai · Reading: LN Ch.5; Case 5

Session 7 · Choosing models by product group — ABC-XYZ, intermittent items, out-of-sample best fit — policies for the whole portfolio

  • Segment the portfolio by ABC (value) × XYZ (variability) — and know when CV misleads
  • Identify demand shapes with ADI and CV² (smooth, erratic, intermittent, lumpy)
  • Use Croston/SBA/TSB for intermittent items and understand what that forecast means
  • Build a method matrix for 24 SKUs
  • Compare top-down and bottom-up; ask the right questions when software reports 'best fit'
  • Write the capstone project proposal

Learning outcomes: ILO5 (portfolio-level forecasting policy) · ILO3 (out-of-sample model selection) · Excel: B07_DanhMuc_ChonMoHinh · Reading: LN 6.10, 6.12, Ch.4, Ch.7; Syntetos & Boylan (2005)

Session 8 · Cross-functional collaboration & Demand Review — FVA, Tracking Signal and a 60-minute Demand Review meeting game

  • Describe what information each department brings to the forecast and which way it tends to be biased
  • Measure Forecast Value Added (FVA): which adjustments help and which hurt
  • Use the Tracking Signal to detect systematic bias
  • Set adjustment rules: who, when, how much, what to record
  • Chair a Demand Review meeting and agree the consensus number
  • Keep an assumption log for the capstone project

Learning outcomes: ILO6 (Demand Review, FVA, challenging with evidence) · Excel: B08_DemandReview_FVA · Reading: LN 6.5, 6.9; Case 6; Gilliland (2010)

Session 9 · From forecast to inventory and S&OP — Safety stock, early warning, shelf life, capacity and 5 KPIs

  • Compute safety stock and the reorder point (ROP) from out-of-sample forecast error
  • Explain the trade-off between service level and inventory capital
  • Build an in-month early warning from the sales rate of the first 14 days
  • Spot lots at shelf-life risk under the FEFO rule
  • Compare unconstrained demand with capacity and propose options
  • Convert volume into value and present 5 KPIs for the S&OP meeting

Learning outcomes: ILO7 (inventory, S&OP, decisions with trade-offs) · Excel: B09_TonKho_SOP · Reading: LN 6.1–6.3, 6.6, 6.9, 6.13; Case 4, Case 9

Session 10 · S&OP meeting simulation & handover — Capstone presentations, surprise events and revealing MBF's 'future' answers

  • Present the 12-month demand plan as in a real S&OP meeting
  • Handle a surprise event in 5 minutes: quantify, choose an option, state the trade-offs
  • Answer the panel's challenging questions with data and assumptions
  • Hand over the unconstrained demand plan to Production Planning
  • Compare your forecast with MBF's 'future' and draw lessons
  • Make a 30–60–90 day plan to apply at your company

Learning outcomes: ILO8 (integrating the full cycle, S&OP presentation, handover) · synthesises ILO1–ILO7 · Excel: B10_MoPhong_SOP · Reading: LN Ch.9, 10.1; Case 6

4. The running case: Mekong Bloom Foods (MBF)

MBF is a fictional food company selling through two channels: GT (general trade: distributors, grocery stores, including HoReCa/B2B) and MT (supermarkets, chains). The data is deliberately generated by a model (fixed seed) so there is a 'true demand' answer key — something real data never has.

Why use controlled, generated data?
  • There is a 'true demand' answer key to grade data cleaning and 12-month forecast accuracy (revealed in Session 10).
  • No company's information is exposed, yet the demand shapes and typical business problems of Vietnamese FMCG are preserved.
  • Real situations from industry experts are woven into the 'Company corner' after anonymisation.

6. Academic integrity, AI use and data confidentiality

6.1 Academic integrity

  • Homework and the capstone are individual work; group discussion is encouraged but the numbers, reasoning and writing must be your own.
  • Cite the source of every formula, figure or idea taken from external material (Harvard referencing).
  • Do not optimise parameters on test data or 'peek' at future data — breaking this rule is treated as a serious method error (the 'Model evaluation' criterion is capped at 1/4).

6.2 Policy on AI tools (ChatGPT, Claude, Copilot…)

AllowedMust be declaredNot allowed
Asking for explanations of concepts; suggesting Excel formulas; writing/fixing Python code; polishing report wordingOne line at the end of the report: which tool, used for what. You are responsible for verifying every number (see the Python manual, 'Safe vibe coding' section)Putting real company data (not anonymised) into public AI tools; submitting numbers you cannot reproduce in the workbook

6.3 Company data (capstone Option B)

Learners who want to use their own company's data for the capstone must anonymise it with the rules below and have it approved by the instructor in HW7:

  • Do: rename products, customers, channels and locations as codes; multiply all quantities and values by one fixed factor that you keep secret; round consistently.
  • Avoid: editing individual values; smoothing or deleting spikes, stock-outs or promotions without recording it; mixing sources/periods; breaking relationships between tables; shifting dates (which misplaces Tết).
  • Must include: a data dictionary, event notes, an adjustment log and confirmation from your line manager that the data may be used for learning.
  • Data is stored only in the authorised class folder; the instructor deletes it once final results are out.

8. Pre-course survey and adapting to company type

A week before Session 1, learners complete a 15-minute survey. The results help the instructor form groups (mixing industries and experience) and choose 'Company corner' examples.

  1. Industry and company type; current role; years of experience.
  2. At what level does your company forecast (SKU, product group, channel, customer) and how often?
  3. Which tools do you use (Excel, which ERP/APS, Python/R)?
  4. Do you measure forecast accuracy? With which metric?
  5. A recent decision affected by a wrong forecast — what happened?
  6. A 10-minute Excel check: SUMIFS, INDEX/MATCH, PivotTable, Solver.
  7. Would you like to use your company's data for the capstone (Option B)?

TermMeaning in this course

GT / MTGeneral trade (distributors, grocery stores) / modern trade (supermarkets, chains)

Train / testThe data used to build the model / the data held back for out-of-sample evaluation (last 6 months)

LagThe gap between when a forecast is frozen and the month being forecast
FVAForecast Value Added — the error reduction contributed by a step in the process

Safety Stockz × out-of-sample error σ × √lead time
