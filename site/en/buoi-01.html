Why forecast demand & forecasting hierarchy

From a forecast number to each department's decisions

Stage 1 · FoundationsClass date: ILO1

By the end of this session you will be able to

  1. Explain which decisions forecasting serves at each horizon (week, month, year)
  2. Clearly distinguish demand, forecast, plan and target, and why mixing them up causes harm
  3. Place forecasting in the planning chain: Forecast → S&OP → MPS → MRP, and the planning time fences
  4. Compute the coefficient of variation CV at 4 levels and choose the right forecasting level for each decision
  5. Recognise the bullwhip effect when each department forecasts on its own
  6. Map departments – decisions – horizons for your own company

Learning outcomes: ILO1 (role of forecasting in the planning system) · foundation for ILO7 (link to S&OP and Production Planning)

Company corner

Who does the forecast number pass through? — How coordination really works in Vietnamese companies — domestic FMCG, B2B/OEM and exporters.

Case · 'November's target is 50,000 cases'
  • Early-October 2026 meeting. The GT Sales Director: 'In November GT must hit 50,000 cases of N01 — that's the target the company set. Just put 50,000 in the forecast.'
  • Your statistical forecast: ~44,000 cases (typical error ±4,500).
  • Factory: in November the packet-noodle line is down for 6 days of maintenance, cutting capacity to ~82,000 cases for the whole packet-noodle group.
  • Finance: the Q4 budget is based on the Sales target.
Discussion questions
  1. Is 50,000 demand, a forecast, a plan or a target? How do you answer the Sales Director in 2 sentences?
  2. If we produce 50,000 and actually sell 44,000: what happens to noodles with a 180-day shelf life?
  3. Which numbers would you record in the meeting minutes (and what would you call them)?
  4. When should the 6,000-case gap be raised at the leadership S&OP meeting?

Groups of 3, one role each. Goal: leave the meeting with ONE forecast number and a list of assumptions.

RoleObjectiveOpening line
GT Sales DirectorDoesn't want to look as if they 'don't believe in the target'; afraid of running short in the peak season“Last November we ran short and lost hundreds of millions. This year we must have extra.”
Demand PlannerDefend an unbiased number, separate the target from the forecast, record assumptions“I agree the target is 50,000. Let's look together at where the extra 6,000 will come from.”
Head of FinanceWants the number to match the Q4 budget; worried about working capital“If the forecast is below budget, how do I report that to the board?”

Key points to remember
  • Forecast ≠ target ≠ plan — call each number by its right name
  • Aggregation stabilises: CV falls from ≈ 0.30 (SKU × channel) to ≈ 0.14 (whole company)
  • The bullwhip comes from behaviour and process — use the data closest to the buyer
